## Dictionary

The dictionary inside Voka is compiled from these sources. Voka contains a table derived from them
— words, how common they are, how they sound and how they inflect — not the sources themselves.

| Source | Used for | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| [CMU Pronouncing Dictionary](https://github.com/cmusphinx/cmudict) | Pronunciations | BSD 2-Clause, Carnegie Mellon University |
| [WordNet](https://wordnet.princeton.edu/) | Parts of speech, capitalisation of proper nouns | WordNet License, Princeton University |
| [UniMorph English](https://github.com/unimorph/eng) | Verb forms, noun plurals | CC BY-SA 3.0 |
| [wordfreq](https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq) (Robyn Speer) | Word frequencies | CC BY-SA 4.0 |
| [FrequencyWords](https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords) (Hermit Dave), counted on [OpenSubtitles](https://www.opensubtitles.org) 2018 | Word frequencies, attested words | CC BY-SA 4.0 |
| [Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.com/tags) tags, through the Stack Exchange API | Programming terms that are left as typed | CC BY-SA 4.0 |
| [pluralize](https://github.com/plurals/pluralize), [irregular-plurals](https://github.com/sindresorhus/irregular-plurals) | Plural forms | MIT |

The short meanings shown next to candidates were written for Voka with the help of language models.
They were not copied from a dictionary. Which acronyms are written in capitals is taken from the
vocabulary of the language model's tokenizer (see below).

Because the word forms are adapted from UniMorph and the word frequencies from wordfreq and
FrequencyWords, the compiled dictionary as a whole is shared under the
[Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)
licence. If you redistribute it, or a dictionary built from it, keep these acknowledgements and use
the same licence.

wordfreq: Robyn Speer. (2022). rspeer/wordfreq: v3.0 (v3.0.2). Zenodo.
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7199437. The sources it combines are credited in its documentation.
One of them is SUBTLEX, which is freely available data: Brysbaert, M., & New, B. (2009). Moving
beyond Kučera and Francis. *Behavior Research Methods*, 41(4), 977–990.

## Language model

The language model inside Voka is a conversion of
[ettin-decoder-17m](https://huggingface.co/jhu-clsp/ettin-decoder-17m), published under the MIT License
by the Center for Language and Speech Processing at Johns Hopkins University and described in *Seq vs
Seq: An Open Suite of Paired Encoders and Decoders* by Orion Weller, Kathryn Ricci, Marc Marone,
Antoine Chaffin, Dawn Lawrie and Benjamin Van Durme (2025).

The tokenizer file that comes with the model (`lm-tokenizer.json`) is the tokenizer of
[ModernBERT](https://huggingface.co/answerdotai/ModernBERT-base) by Answer.AI and LightOn, published
under the Apache License 2.0. It is included unchanged.
